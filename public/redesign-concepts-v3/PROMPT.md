# TezHR marketing redesign — “Clear Signal”

## Improved master prompt

Redesign TezHR’s marketing website as one coherent, Awwwards-calibre corporate product system—not a visual experiment and not a generic AI SaaS template.

TezHR is an applicant-tracking system built for hiring in Uzbekistan. It turns large, multilingual applicant pools into a clear, evidence-backed shortlist in seconds. It understands Uzbek, Russian, and English, sources candidates from branded application pages, an owned talent pool, hh.uz, and public Telegram channels, and always leaves the final decision to a human recruiter.

Create a consistent set of high-fidelity 1536 × 1024 desktop marketing-page concepts. Balance approximately 70% commercial clarity with 30% creative distinction. Every page must feel unmistakably TezHR and part of the same website.

### Creative direction

- Central brand idea: **from applicant noise to a clear hiring signal**.
- One dominant idea and one primary product proof per page. Never stack several visual metaphors in one viewport.
- Use generous negative space, calm pacing, a disciplined 12-column grid, and no more than two major compositional zones above the fold.
- Keep a consistent slim header across all pages: TezHR mark and wordmark; Product, Sourcing, Security, About; RU / UZ / EN; and a restrained “Start free” button.
- Use a warm near-white canvas (#F5F6F2), deep ink (#101820), signal blue (#176BFF), and very limited human amber (#F26A2E) only where recruiter judgement is represented.
- Use one modern neo-grotesk family: regular, highly readable body copy plus a mildly condensed bold display cut. Tiny operational labels may use a neutral mono. Avoid luxury serif styling and extreme condensed poster typography.
- Use a single recurring signature element: a precise blue **signal path** that organizes information or leads the eye. Keep it thin, functional, and different in composition—but identical in visual language—on every page.
- Show believable ATS product evidence: realistic candidate names from Uzbekistan, roles, languages, match percentages, evidence excerpts, sources, and recruiter actions. Product UI should feel integrated into the composition, not a collection of floating glass cards.
- Use restrained radii, hairline rules only where structurally necessary, subtle depth, and almost no texture. Creativity must come from composition, typography, data choreography, and product relevance.
- Copy must be short, specific, and truthful. Never imply automatic rejection. Do not invent customers, testimonials, awards, or unsupported business metrics.

### Avoid

Generic AI gradients, glowing orbs, robots, brains, sparkles, cosmic imagery, purple-blue haze, excessive borders, card grids, bento overload, glassmorphism, random 3D objects, paper collage, torn edges, poster density, decorative dashboards, fake logos, fake testimonials, long paragraphs, and inconsistent navigation or typography.

### Output quality

Polished editorial art direction; exact alignment; realistic product UI; crisp typography; desktop browser viewport; no browser chrome; no device mockup; no watermark. The result should look ready for a senior design review and credible for an operating B2B product.

## Page set

1. Home — “The shortlist, without the longlist.” Show a large but quiet field of applicant rows resolving into a five-person shortlist.
2. Product — “Every ranking has a reason.” Show one candidate evidence view with strengths, gaps, language evidence, and a clear human decision control.
3. Sourcing — “One role. Every relevant source.” Show a source-to-pipeline map for branded pages, talent pool, hh.uz, and public Telegram.
4. Local intelligence — “Built for how Uzbekistan hires.” Show multilingual evidence in Uzbek, Russian, and English connected to a single candidate record.
5. Security — “Hiring data stays in your hands.” Show a calm access-and-custody view with OTP, roles, audit trail, and human control.
6. About — “Built in Tashkent for the teams building next.” Use an authentic, contemporary Tashkent workplace/city composition with a restrained product overlay.
7. Contact — “Bring us the role that is slowing you down.” Create an editorial two-column contact/demo page with a concise form and a useful pre-call checklist.
